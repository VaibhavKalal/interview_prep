# 13 — Behavioral, Leadership & Mock Loops (Week 14, but start stories in Week 2)

**Why it matters:** at senior level, the behavioral round often decides between offers and
down-levels. Interviewers look for *scope, ownership, influence, and judgment*.

Start writing stories early (one per week from week 2), so by week 14 you're polishing,
not writing from scratch.

## Senior signals interviewers look for
- **Scope:** your work affected a team or system, not just a ticket
- **Ownership:** you saw problems through, including production and the unglamorous parts
- **Judgment:** you made tradeoffs consciously and can explain the alternatives you rejected
- **Influence:** you changed other people's minds or practices without authority
- **Multiplying others:** mentoring, code reviews, docs, better processes
- **Handling ambiguity:** you turned a vague problem into a plan

## Checklist
- [ ] 8 STAR stories written in `stories/` (see the checklist there)
- [ ] Each story has numbers (latency, cost, incidents, time saved, users)
- [ ] Each story told out loud in under 2.5 minutes
- [ ] A 2-minute "tell me about yourself" pitch, ending with why senior/why this role
- [ ] A 5-minute deep technical walk-through of the notification system (architecture, your decisions, what went wrong, what you'd change)
- [ ] 5 good questions to ask interviewers
- [ ] Mock loop 1: coding + system design + behavioral (with Claude or a friend)
- [ ] Mock loop 2: same, different problems
- [ ] Mock loop 3: a company-style loop for your top target company

## Reading
- *The Staff Engineer's Path* — Tanya Reilly (for vocabulary on scope and influence; useful even at senior level)
- *The Manager's Path* — Camille Fournier: the tech lead chapters

## Self-test
- "Tell me about the most technically complex thing you've built." Can you go 3 levels deep on follow-ups?
- "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a technical decision." Does your answer show both respect and conviction?
- "What would you do differently?" Do you have an honest answer?
